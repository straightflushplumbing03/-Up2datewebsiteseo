Slab leaks in San Clemente homes

San Clemente's homes span a century. Near the pier and downtown you'll find older Spanish-style houses, many with original copper; east of the 5, neighborhoods like Forster Ranch and Talega are newer, typically built on concrete slabs with copper water lines run under the concrete. A slab leak can happen in either.

Newer doesn't mean immune. In 2015 a lawsuit over the 3,800-home Talega community claimed its copper pipe was prone to leaks under local water conditions (PHCP Pros), and ABC7 reported pinhole-leak claims over 2003–2013 homes in nearby Ladera Ranch (ABC7). Those are claims, not findings about your house — the only way to know is to test it.

Water supply matters too. Most of the city is served by the City of San Clemente's utility, Talega by Santa Margarita Water District, and parts of north San Clemente by South Coast Water District. The water is hard, so scale shows up in water heaters and fixtures. If you suspect a leak, start with the 30-minute water meter test: a meter that keeps moving with every fixture and irrigation zone off means water is escaping somewhere.

How we work

Locate it precisely before anything is opened

Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing confirms whether the hot or cold line is losing water, and which zone of the home it runs through.

2 · Pinpoint

Acoustic and thermal

We listen for the leak itself through the slab and use thermal imaging to cross-check hot-water leaks, then mark the spot for the smallest possible opening.

When a camera inspection is worth it

A single slow sink is usually a local clog and doesn't need a camera. A camera earns its keep when:

  • The same drain or toilet backs up again after being cleared
  • Several fixtures drain slowly or gurgle at once, or water comes up in a shower when a toilet flushes
  • There is sewer odor indoors or a soggy, smelly spot in the yard
  • Large trees grow near the sewer line
  • You're buying an older home and want to know the condition of the line before closing

Your sewer lateral is yours

The pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the public sewer is the private sewer lateral. The City of Laguna Niguel's sewage spill guide for property owners says property owners are responsible for maintaining their private laterals from the home to the connection with the public sewer, which can include areas under the sidewalk and the street. Most South County sewer agencies draw the line the same way, so check with yours. That is why it pays to know the lateral's condition before it fails.

The same guide warns that tree roots find their way into cracked or broken laterals and cause blockages, that fats, oils and grease from household drains build up in the lateral, and that a deteriorated, missing or broken cleanout cap is a problem in its own right.

If sewage is backing up now

Stop using water in the house and, if needed, turn off the main water supply. If sewage reaches a gutter or storm drain, report it to the Orange County 24-hour pollution hotline at 1-877-89-SPILL (1-877-897-7455) and to your sewer agency, then call a licensed plumber.

What the camera can show
  • Tree root intrusion at joints and cracks
  • Cracked, broken or collapsed pipe
  • Offset or separated joints
  • Sags (bellies) where water and waste collect
  • Grease and scale buildup narrowing the pipe
  • Where the line runs and how deep, for any repair that follows

Where leaks show up in Ladera Ranch homes

Pinholes in original copper. Ladera Ranch broke ground in 1999. Starting in 2013, hundreds of homeowners sued their builders over pinhole leaks in copper water pipes, and one settlement covered homes built between May 2003 and May 2013 (ABC7; PM Magazine). The cause was disputed. If your home still has its original copper, a pinhole inside a wall or under the slab is the first thing to rule out. More on slab leak detection.

Fittings and connections after a PEX repipe. Many Ladera homes have been repiped since. In those homes we look hardest at fittings, fixture connections, water heater connectors and toilets. Santa Margarita Water District calls toilets the number one cause of indoor leaks and notes a leaking toilet can be silent (SMWD).

Irrigation and the line from the meter. Sprinkler valves, drip lines and the buried line from the meter to the house can lose water with no sign indoors except the bill or a patch that stays soggy. If your yard is watered by purple-pipe recycled water, that is a separate system from the drinking water in your home, so a leak on one does not show on the other.

Pressure and hard water. SMWD notes pressure over 65 PSI can strain irrigation parts, and the pressure regulator on your incoming line is the homeowner's, not the district's (SMWD FAQ). Ladera water is also hard, about 15 grains per gallon (SMWD), which wears water heaters, shutoff valves and faucet cartridges.

Check for a leak yourself first

Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone, note the reading on your water meter, and check again after 30 minutes. SMWD puts it simply: the meter dials should be motionless when no water is being used (SMWD). If the meter moved, water is leaving the system somewhere. Our water meter test guide walks through it. If your bill is very high, SMWD also offers a home water audit, and it offers rebates on qualifying flow-monitoring devices that send leak alerts (SMWD).

How we work

Find the leak first, then a straight answer on the fix

1 · Isolate

Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing shows whether the hot line, cold line, the service line from the meter or a yard line is losing water, so we know where to look.

2 · Pinpoint

Listen, trace and confirm

Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping, electronic locating traces where the pipe runs, and thermal imaging cross-checks hot-water leaks. The spot is marked for the smallest possible opening.

3 · Decide

Repair, reroute, or repipe

A single leak in a sound line is usually a spot repair. Repeat leaks in aging copper may make a reroute or repipe the smarter call. We explain the trade-offs after diagnosis, never before.

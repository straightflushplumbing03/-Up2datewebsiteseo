Why Ladera Ranch homes leak

Pinhole leaks in original copper. Ladera Ranch broke ground in 1999, so its homes are younger than most of South Orange County, but newer has not meant leak-free. Starting in 2013, hundreds of Ladera Ranch homeowners sued their builders over pinhole leaks in copper water pipes, and one settlement covered homes built between May 2003 and May 2013 (ABC7; PM Magazine). The cause was disputed: homeowners pointed to how the treated water reacts with the copper, and builders said the pipe itself was not defective. If your home still has its original copper, a pinhole leak inside a wall or under the slab is the first thing to rule out.

Already repiped with PEX? Many Ladera homes have been. In those homes, leaks are more likely to come from fittings, fixture connections, the water heater, appliance supply lines or irrigation, so we test those instead of assuming the pipe failed. If you are weighing a repipe, see PEX repiping in Ladera Ranch.

Hard water. The Santa Margarita Water District's 2026 water quality report, which covers Ladera Ranch, lists average hardness in the district system at 256 mg/L, about 15 grains per gallon (SMWD). Scale wears on water heaters, shutoff valves and faucet cartridges, which is why those get checked alongside the pipes. Scale by itself does not mean you have a hidden leak.

Irrigation lines. The purple-pipe recycled-water lines used for irrigation are a separate system from the drinking water in your home. A leak on the household side shows on your water meter; a soggy patch near a sprinkler line can be either, and testing tells them apart.

Check for a leak yourself first

Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone, note the reading on your water meter, and check again after 30 minutes. If it moved, water is leaving the system somewhere. Our water meter test guide walks through it, and why is my water bill so high? covers the usual causes.

Whose leak is it? District, county and HOA

Ladera Ranch water comes from the Santa Margarita Water District (SMWD), customer care (949) 459-6420. A leak in the street or on the district's side of the meter goes to SMWD. A leak on your side of the meter, in the line to the house, under the slab or inside a wall, is generally the owner's to find and fix, and that is the part we locate.

Ladera Ranch is not a city. It is an unincorporated community, so building permits come from the County of Orange; OC Public Works lists plumbing systems among the work that typically needs a permit. The Ladera Ranch Maintenance Corporation (LARMAC) and your village association sit alongside that, so check with them before work that touches shared areas or the exterior.

How we work

Find the leak first, then a straight answer on the fix

1 · Isolate

Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing shows whether the hot line, cold line or the service line from the meter is losing water, so we know where to look.

2 · Pinpoint

Listen, trace and confirm

Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping, electronic locating traces where the pipe runs, and thermal imaging cross-checks hot-water leaks. The spot is marked for the smallest possible opening.

3 · Decide

Repair, reroute, or repipe

A single leak in a sound line is usually a spot repair. Repeat leaks in aging copper may make a reroute or repipe the smarter call. We explain the trade-offs after diagnosis, never before.

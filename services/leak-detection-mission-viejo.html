Inside walls and ceilings. Supply lines to bathrooms, kitchens and laundry rooms run behind drywall, and in two-story homes a leak upstairs often first appears as a ceiling stain below.

Check for a leak yourself first

Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone, note the reading on your water meter, and check again after 30 minutes. If it moved, water is leaving the system somewhere. Our water meter test guide walks through it, and why is my water bill so high? covers the usual causes.

How we work

Find the leak first, then a straight answer on the fix

1 · Isolate

Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing shows whether the hot line, cold line or the service line from the meter is losing water, so we know where to look.

2 · Pinpoint

Listen, trace and confirm

Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping, electronic locating traces where the pipe runs, and thermal imaging cross-checks hot-water leaks. The spot is marked for the smallest possible opening.

3 · Decide

Repair, reroute, or repipe

A single leak in a sound line is usually a spot repair. Repeat leaks in aging or failing copper may make a reroute or repipe the smarter call. We explain the trade-offs after diagnosis, never before.

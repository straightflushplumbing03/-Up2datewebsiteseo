Ladera Ranch's copper pinhole-leak history

Ladera Ranch broke ground in 1999, so most homes are newer than the rest of South Orange County. Even so, starting in 2013 hundreds of Ladera Ranch homeowners sued their builders over pinhole leaks in copper water pipes. Settlements reached in 2015 and 2016 paid toward PEX repiping or epoxy pipe treatment for covered homes built between May 2003 and May 2013 (ABC7, 2016; plaintiffs' counsel, 2015).

The cause was never settled as a finding. Homeowners argued the copper was not suited to the local water; builders said there was no defect in the pipe itself. A separate class action against the water districts was dismissed in 2015, with the court ruling that districts following state and federal disinfectant standards could not be sued (report of the July 2015 ruling). Ladera Ranch water is supplied by the Santa Margarita Water District.

What that means for you: if your home still has its original copper and you have had one pinhole leak, another one is worth planning for. If a previous owner already repiped with PEX, leaks are more likely to come from fittings, fixture connections, the water heater or irrigation lines.

Still have copper? A 2-minute check

Open the cabinet under a kitchen or bathroom sink and look where the supply pipes leave the wall, then look at the pipes on top of your water heater. Reddish-brown metal is copper. Flexible plastic tubing in red, blue or white is PEX. Green or blue crust on copper fittings is worth mentioning when you call.

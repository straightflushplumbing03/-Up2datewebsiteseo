Slab leaks in Dana Point homes

Dana Point mixes older homes near the harbor, many with original copper plumbing, with newer hillside and gated communities. In the older homes, copper lines running under the slab are the usual source of a slab leak; on hillside lots, soil movement adds stress where the pipe meets concrete.

Salt air and coastal humidity also mean exposed pipe, valves and fittings corrode faster here. When water shows up where it shouldn't, we confirm whether it's coming from a line under the slab, an exposed fitting, or something else entirely — guesswork tends to cost more in Dana Point than elsewhere.

If you suspect a leak, start with the 30-minute water meter test. A meter that keeps moving with every fixture and irrigation zone off means water is escaping somewhere in the system.

How we work

Locate it precisely before anything is opened

1 · Isolate

Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing confirms whether the hot or cold line is losing water, and which zone of the home it runs through.

2 · Pinpoint

Acoustic and thermal

We listen for the leak itself through the slab and use thermal imaging to cross-check hot-water leaks, then mark the spot for the smallest possible opening.

3 · Decide

Repair, reroute, or repipe

A single leak in sound pipe is often a spot repair; repeated leaks in aging copper may justify a reroute or repipe. You get the options and trade-offs before any work starts.

Talking to insurance about a slab leak or pipe leak: do's and don'ts

If you remember only one thing: the less you say, the better. You are not the diagnostician. Your plumber is diagnosing the problem and will write the report. Put the technical details on that report — not on a phone call where you are guessing.

This page is practical guidance from a working plumber, not legal or insurance advice. Every policy is different. Your carrier decides coverage. When in doubt, ask your agent in writing and rely on your plumber’s written findings.

The #1 rule: say less

Insurance companies evaluate claims carefully. They are looking for reasons a loss may not be covered — especially long-term seepage, wear and tear, or lack of maintenance. Homeowners who speculate on the phone (“it might have been leaking for months,” “I think the copper is just old,” “we ignored a high bill for a while”) can accidentally hurt their own claim.

You do not need to diagnose the leak. You need to report that there is a problem, stop further damage if you can, hire a plumber, and let the written report document what was found.

What to say (simple scripts)

When your agent or adjuster asks what is going on:

  • “I’m not sure yet. I’m not a plumber.”
  • “My plumber is still diagnosing it and will write a report. I can forward that to you.”
  • “If you have technical questions, you’re welcome to contact my plumber directly.”

That is enough. You are not being difficult — you are being accurate. Until the diagnosis is finished, you genuinely do not know the full picture.

What not to say

  • Do not guess how long the leak has been active.
  • Do not invent a cause (“it’s definitely the soil,” “it’s been dripping for years”).
  • Do not argue policy language on the first call. Get the report first.
  • Do not minimize or exaggerate damage. Stick to what you can see: wet area, high bill, warm floor, etc.
  • Do not authorize exploratory demolition before you understand the diagnosis and your options.

Why the plumber’s report is so important

At the end of the day, the written report from the plumbing company that located the leak is one of the most important documents in the claim. Adjusters and carriers lean on clear findings: where the leak is, what type of failure was found, and what was observed. A solid report is far more useful than a long conversation where the homeowner tries to explain plumbing.

Straight Flush provides clear diagnostic documentation homeowners can forward to their insurance company. If the carrier has questions, they can contact us.

Sudden failure vs. long-term issues (why wording matters)

Many homeowner policies treat sudden and accidental water damage differently from long-term seepage, gradual corrosion, or neglected maintenance. That is why homeowners should not casually say a leak “must have been going on forever” unless that is actually established.

Do not invent a story. Do let your plumber complete the diagnosis and put accurate findings in writing. If your plumber describes a sudden pipe failure or burst-type event based on what was found, that language belongs in the professional report — not in a speculative phone call from you before anyone has opened or tested anything.

Key idea: you are not the expert on the call; the report is.

What insurance often covers vs. does not

Patterns we see with South Orange County homeowners (always confirm with your carrier):

  • Often discussed as covered (when the policy allows): water damage to finishes, flooring, drywall, and similar resulting damage from a covered water event.
  • Often not covered: the plumbing repair itself (the pipe, the slab cut, the repipe). Many policies exclude the system that failed and focus on resulting damage.
  • Leak detection: Many carriers will reimburse the homeowner for a professional leak detection visit from a plumber of the homeowner’s choice so the cause can be identified. It is not universal — ask. Keep your invoice and report.

None of this is a promise of coverage. Your declarations page and policy language control.

Do's checklist

  • Do stop the water if you can and limit further damage.
  • Do call a plumber who will diagnose first and document findings in writing.
  • Do tell your agent you have a water issue and a plumber is diagnosing it.
  • Do forward the plumber’s report when it is ready.
  • Do keep photos of visible damage and copies of invoices.
  • Do ask your carrier whether leak detection is reimbursable under your policy.
  • Do invite the adjuster to contact the plumber for technical questions.

Don'ts checklist

  • Don’t speculate about cause or timeline on the phone.
  • Don’t say you “ignored it for months” if you are not sure — you may not know when it started.
  • Don’t try to sound like a plumbing expert.
  • Don’t approve major demolition before diagnosis and a clear plan.
  • Don’t discard damaged materials before the adjuster has what they need (ask your carrier).
  • Don’t assume the pipe repair is covered the same way as the water damage.

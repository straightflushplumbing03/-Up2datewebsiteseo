Slab leaks

Slab Leak Detection in Dove Canyon

A slab leak is a failure in a water line that runs through or beneath a home's concrete foundation. Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water with everything shut off, a spike in the water or gas bill, or unexplained moisture at the base of a wall are the reasons homeowners call us to investigate. Not every one of those symptoms turns out to be a slab leak, and that is precisely why the diagnostic step matters.

Slab leaks are not the most common problem in Dove Canyon, and it is worth saying so plainly. The housing here is developed in the 1980s and 1990s, and the calls we actually take most often involve long private service runs where a leak surfaces far from its source. A slab leak is still possible, but the odds should shape what gets tested first.

Detection means confirming the location first: acoustic equipment to listen for the signature of pressurized water escaping, thermal imaging to look for the temperature difference a hot-water line creates, and pressure testing to establish which line is losing water. Only once the leak is located does it make sense to discuss the repair itself. See slab leak detection for the process.

Water lines

Water Line Leak Detection & Repair

Water service lines and pressurized supply lines carry water under constant pressure, so a failure can lose a significant volume of water before it ever reaches the surface. Underground service lines between the meter and the house are a common source, particularly where soil movement or age has stressed the pipe.

This is one of the more frequent calls we get in Dove Canyon. The large-lot gated estate pattern here means water can travel a long way from the failure before it shows, so tracing it rather than digging at the first wet patch saves real money.

The useful first step is establishing whether the loss is on the pressurized supply side or the drainage side, and whether it is inside the structure or outside it. Straight Flush diagnoses pressurized water line leaks, including underground lines, before recommending repair or rerouting. Related: plumbing repair and emergency plumbing.

Repiping

Whole-House Repiping in Dove Canyon

When a home has had several leaks in a relatively short period, the question shifts from “how do we fix this one?” to “how much of this piping is near the end of its service life?” Repeated pinhole failures on aging copper are the most common reason homeowners start evaluating whole-house repiping rather than continuing to repair one section at a time.

Most homes in Dove Canyon are not there yet, because the housing is developed in the 1980s and 1990s. When repiping does come up here, it is usually driven by a pattern of failures rather than age on its own.

Repiping is not automatically the right answer, and it is not a decision to make under pressure. Straight Flush walks Dove Canyon homeowners through the honest options — a targeted repair, a reroute around the failing section, or full repiping in PEX — with the trade-offs of each, so the choice matches the actual condition of the plumbing.

 Before you call


## Simple repair, or a sign of a hidden leak?



Most fixture problems are exactly what they look like. A few quick checks tell you whether you need an everyday repair or [leak detection](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/services/leak-detection.html):




- **Toilet dye test.** The EPA suggests putting a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank. If color shows up in the bowl after 10 minutes without flushing, the flapper or valve is leaking. Flush afterward so the tank doesn’t stain.

- **Meter test.** Turn off every fixture and appliance and watch the water meter. If it still moves, the leak may be somewhere you can’t see. Here’s [what a moving meter means](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/water-meter-still-moving-everything-off.html).

- **Pressure check.** The California Plumbing Code (section 608.2) requires a pressure regulator when static water pressure is above 80 psi, reducing it to 80 psi or less. Pressure that is too high wears out faucet cartridges, toilet fill valves and water heater parts faster.




If the problem is a part you can see, it is usually an everyday repair. If the meter moves with everything off, or you hear [running water in a wall](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/sound-of-running-water-in-wall.html), the next step is finding the leak before anything gets opened up.

## Pressure problems aren't always where you'd expect



We've traced pressure issues on buried lines back to a pressure regulator installed on the wrong end of the run — downstream instead of at the source. Moving it upstream solved what looked, at first, like a much bigger problem. That's the value of diagnosing before repairing: sometimes the fix is smaller than it looks, and sometimes it's in a different place than expected.

 Plumbing repair FAQ


## Common questions



 Why does my water pressure keep dropping? +


Common causes include a failing pressure regulator (PRV), mineral buildup in pipes, or a hidden leak somewhere in the system. We diagnose which one applies before recommending a fix.




 Is a running toilet worth fixing right away? +


Yes. The EPA notes that a running toilet can waste 200 gallons of water or more every day, so it shows up on your water bill quickly. The fix is usually a worn flapper or fill valve, which is typically a small repair.




 Do you repair or only replace fixtures? +


We repair whenever a repair is the sound long-term choice, and only recommend replacement when the fixture or part is genuinely beyond a reasonable repair.




 How can I tell if my toilet is leaking? +


Use the EPA dye test: put a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank and wait 10 minutes without flushing. If color shows up in the bowl, water is leaking from the tank, usually past the flapper. Flush afterward so the tank doesn’t stain.




 Is a dripping faucet really worth repairing? +


Yes. According to the EPA, a faucet dripping at one drip per second can waste more than 3,000 gallons a year. Most drips come from a worn cartridge, washer or seal, and we repair the faucet when that’s the sound choice.




 What water pressure is too high for a house? +


The California Plumbing Code (section 608.2) requires a pressure regulator when static water pressure is above 80 psi, so the house runs at 80 psi or less. If your pressure is higher, or a regulator has failed, fixtures and water heater parts wear out faster. We test the pressure and check the regulator before recommending anything.

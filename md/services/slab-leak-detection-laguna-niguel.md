## Slab leaks in Laguna Niguel homes



Much of Laguna Niguel was built in the 1970s–90s, from Bear Brand to Niguel Summit, and a large share of those homes still run their original copper water lines through or under the slab. Decades of mineral-rich water, pipe rubbing against concrete or rebar, and South OC's expansive clay soils are the usual reasons those lines eventually pinhole.



The early signs are easy to miss: a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water with everything off, a water bill that jumps for no reason, or a water meter that keeps moving. Laguna Niguel is served by Moulton Niguel Water District, so your bill history there is a good place to look for an unexplained rise in use.



Before you call anyone, you can run the [30-minute water meter test](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/water-meter-still-moving-everything-off.html). If the meter moves with every fixture and irrigation zone off, water is leaving the system somewhere — and that's where acoustic detection comes in.

 How we work


## Find the leak first, then a straight answer on repair, reroute or repipe



 1 · Isolate

### Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing confirms whether the hot or cold line is losing water, and which part of the house it runs through.


 2 · Pinpoint

### Listen for the leak itself

Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping under the slab; thermal imaging cross-checks hot-water leaks. The spot is marked for the smallest possible opening.


 3 · Decide

### Repair, reroute, or repipe

One leak in otherwise sound copper is often a spot repair. Repeat leaks in aging copper may make a reroute or repipe the smarter call — we explain the trade-offs after diagnosis, never before.

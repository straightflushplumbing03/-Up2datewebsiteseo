## Why Rancho Santa Margarita homes leak



**Plumbing that is now 25 to 40 years old.** The first homes in the Rancho Santa Margarita planned community were sold in 1986, and the city incorporated on January 1, 2000 ([City of RSM General Plan](https://www.cityofrsm.org/DocumentCenter/View/5467/Introduction)). That puts a lot of original copper in its fourth decade, the age where pinhole leaks in walls and under the slab start to show up.



**Foothill lots.** The city sits on the Plano Trabuco plateau in the foothills of the Santa Ana Mountains ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Santa_Margarita,_California)). Homes built into sloped lots can see more soil movement than flatland tracts, which is worth understanding before assuming every slab leak is just old pipe.



**Hard water.** The Santa Margarita Water District's 2026 water quality report lists average hardness in the district system at 256 mg/L, about 15 grains per gallon, with a range of 12.3 to 17.5 grains ([SMWD 2026 report](https://smwd.com/DocumentCenter/View/6349/2026-Water-Quality-Report)). Scale wears on water heaters, shutoff valves and faucet cartridges, so those get checked alongside the pipes.



**The line from the meter.** The buried service line between the meter and the house can leak for months with no sign indoors except the bill and a meter that keeps moving. That is exactly what we found in a Rancho Santa Margarita home this fall (below).



## Check for a leak yourself first



Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone, note the reading on your water meter, and check again after 30 minutes. If it moved, water is leaving the system somewhere. Our [water meter test guide](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/water-meter-still-moving-everything-off.html) walks through it, and [why is my water bill so high?](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/why-is-my-water-bill-so-high.html) covers the usual causes.



## Two water districts: check your bill



Most of the city is served by the Santa Margarita Water District (customer care (949) 459-6420), but eastern neighborhoods, including Robinson Ranch, Trabuco Highlands and Dove Canyon, are served by the Trabuco Canyon Water District instead ([City of RSM General Plan](https://www.cityofrsm.org/DocumentCenter/View/4207/Sec-05_18_Wastewater)). Your bill names your district. A leak in the street or on the district's side of the meter goes to them; a leak on your side of the meter is generally the owner's to find and fix. If a leak ran up your bill, ask your district about its adjustment policy and keep the written findings and repair invoice.

 How we work


## Find the leak first, then a straight answer on the fix



 1 · Isolate

### Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing shows whether the hot line, cold line or the service line from the meter is losing water, so we know where to look.


 2 · Pinpoint

### Listen, trace and confirm

Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping, electronic locating traces where the pipe runs, and thermal imaging cross-checks hot-water leaks. The spot is marked for the smallest possible opening.


 3 · Decide

### Repair, reroute, or repipe

A single leak in a sound line is usually a spot repair. Repeat leaks in aging copper may make a reroute or repipe the smarter call. We explain the trade-offs after diagnosis, never before.

## Ladera Ranch's copper pinhole-leak history



Ladera Ranch broke ground in 1999, so most homes are newer than the rest of South Orange County. Even so, starting in 2013 hundreds of Ladera Ranch homeowners sued their builders over pinhole leaks in copper water pipes. Settlements reached in 2015 and 2016 paid toward PEX repiping or epoxy pipe treatment for covered homes built between May 2003 and May 2013 ([ABC7, 2016](https://abc7.com/post/oc-homes-could-face-possible-damaging-leaks-from-holes-in-pipes/1208620/); [plaintiffs' counsel, 2015](https://www.bridgfordlaw.com/news/bridgford-gleason-artinian-llp-secures-win-for-ladera-homeowners-in-copper-pipe-suit/)).



The cause was never settled as a finding. Homeowners argued the copper was not suited to the local water; builders said there was no defect in the pipe itself. A separate class action against the water districts was dismissed in 2015, with the court ruling that districts following state and federal disinfectant standards could not be sued ([report of the July 2015 ruling](https://www.bridgfordlaw.com/news/judge-tosses-out-lawsuit-on-south-county-copper-pipe-leaks/)). Ladera Ranch water is supplied by the [Santa Margarita Water District](https://smwd.com/627/Water-Quality-Ladera-Ranch).



What that means for you: if your home still has its original copper and you have had one pinhole leak, another one is worth planning for. If a previous owner already repiped with PEX, leaks are more likely to come from fittings, fixture connections, the water heater or irrigation lines.

## Still have copper? A 2-minute check



Open the cabinet under a kitchen or bathroom sink and look where the supply pipes leave the wall, then look at the pipes on top of your water heater. Reddish-brown metal is copper. Flexible plastic tubing in red, blue or white is PEX. Green or blue crust on copper fittings is worth mentioning when you call.

 What causes pinhole leaks in copper pipes? +


Pinhole leaks come from pitting corrosion, where small spots on the inside of a copper pipe wear through while the rest of the pipe looks fine. Water chemistry, water velocity and installation quality are all factors that have been argued. In Ladera Ranch the cause was contested in court and never settled as a finding, so we diagnose each home on its own evidence.




 How do I know if my Ladera Ranch home still has copper pipes? +


Look at the pipes where they come out of the wall under a kitchen or bathroom sink and at the water heater connections. Copper is reddish-brown metal; PEX is flexible plastic tubing, usually red, blue or white. Many Ladera Ranch homes were repiped with PEX after the lawsuits, so a previous owner may already have done it. If you are not sure, we can check during a visit.




 Do I need a whole-house repipe after one pinhole leak in Ladera Ranch? +


Not necessarily. A single leak can often be repaired or a line rerouted. Repeat pinhole leaks in different spots, a leak under the slab, or green or blue staining on exposed copper are signs the system as a whole is wearing, and that is when a whole-house PEX repipe is worth comparing against further repairs. We locate the leak first and lay out both options.




 Is PEX better than copper for Ladera Ranch homes? +


PEX does not corrode the way copper does, and its flexible runs need fewer fittings inside walls. That removes the pitting corrosion behind most pinhole leaks, though no plumbing is leak-proof forever, and fittings, fixture connections and water heaters still need attention. Our copper vs. PEX guide covers the trade-offs.

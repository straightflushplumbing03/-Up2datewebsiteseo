## Where leaks show up in Dana Point homes



**Exposed pipe and fittings near the coast.** Salt air and humidity speed up corrosion on outdoor pipe, hose bibs, valves and the fittings at the water heater. A slow drip there is easy to see once you look; the job is confirming it's the only source.



**Under the slab.** Many older homes near the harbor still run original copper under the concrete. A warm spot on the floor or the sound of running water with everything off are typical signs. Details on our [Dana Point slab leak page](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/services/slab-leak-detection-dana-point.html).



**Inside walls and ceilings.** Supply lines to bathrooms, kitchens and laundry rooms run behind drywall, and in two-story homes a leak upstairs often first appears as a ceiling stain below.



**Between the meter and the house, and on hillside lots.** The buried service line and irrigation lines can leak for a long time with no sign indoors except the bill. A soggy patch, or a spot that stays green in a dry month, can point to it.



## Check for a leak yourself first



Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone, note the reading on your water meter, and check again after 30 minutes. If it moved, water is leaving the system somewhere.  How we work


## Find the leak first, then a straight answer on the fix



 1 · Isolate

### Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing shows whether the hot line, cold line or the service line from the meter is losing water, so we know where to look.


 2 · Pinpoint

### Listen, trace and confirm

Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping, electronic locating traces where the pipe runs, and thermal imaging cross-checks hot-water leaks. The spot is marked for the smallest possible opening.


 3 · Decide

### Repair, reroute, or repipe

A single leak in a sound line is usually a spot repair. Repeat leaks in aging copper may make a reroute or repipe the smarter call. We explain the trade-offs after diagnosis, never before.

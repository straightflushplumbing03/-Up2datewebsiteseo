## When a camera inspection is worth it



A single slow sink is usually a local clog and doesn't need a camera. A camera earns its keep when:




- The same drain or toilet backs up again after being cleared

- Several fixtures drain slowly or gurgle at once, or water comes up in a shower when a toilet flushes

- There is sewer odor indoors or a soggy, smelly spot in the yard

- Large trees grow near the sewer line

- You're buying an older home and want to know the condition of the line before closing




## Your sewer lateral is yours



The pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the public sewer is the private sewer lateral. The City of Laguna Niguel's [sewage spill guide for property owners](https://ca-lagunaniguel3.civicplus.com/DocumentCenter/View/29057/Sewage-Spill-Reference-Guide-for-Private-Property-OwnersLNpdf) says property owners are responsible for maintaining their private laterals from the home to the connection with the public sewer, which can include areas under the sidewalk and the street. Most South County sewer agencies draw the line the same way, so check with yours. That is why it pays to know the lateral's condition before it fails.



The same guide warns that tree roots find their way into cracked or broken laterals and cause blockages, that fats, oils and grease from household drains build up in the lateral, and that a deteriorated, missing or broken cleanout cap is a problem in its own right.



## If sewage is backing up now



Stop using water in the house and, if needed, turn off the main water supply. If sewage reaches a gutter or storm drain, report it to the Orange County 24-hour pollution hotline at 1-877-89-SPILL (1-877-897-7455) and to your sewer agency, then call a licensed plumber.

 Do I need a camera inspection for a slow drain? +


Not always. A single, isolated slow drain is often solved with standard cleaning. A camera inspection is worth it when the problem keeps coming back, affects several fixtures at once, or involves the main sewer line.




 Who is responsible for the sewer line between my house and the street? +


Usually you are. The City of Laguna Niguel's guide for property owners says owners are responsible for maintaining their private sewer laterals from the home to the connection with the public sewer, which can include areas under the sidewalk and the street. Check with your own sewer agency for its exact rules.




 Should I get a sewer camera inspection before buying a house? +


For an older home, or one with large trees near the sewer line, it is a smart check. A camera shows roots, cracks, sags or a collapsed section that a standard home inspection doesn't see, and you learn about it before closing instead of after.




 What causes sewer line backups? +


Common causes are tree roots entering through cracks and joints, grease buildup, aging or broken pipe, sags where waste collects, and things that shouldn't be flushed. The camera shows which one you're dealing with.




 What should I do if sewage is backing up into my house? +


Stop using water and, if needed, turn off the main water supply. If sewage reaches a gutter or storm drain, report it to the Orange County 24-hour pollution hotline at 1-877-89-SPILL (1-877-897-7455) and to your sewer agency, then call a licensed plumber.

 Warning signs


## Signs of a slab leak




- A warm or hot spot on the floor with no obvious cause

- The sound of running water when everything is off

- A noticeable rise in your water bill

- Cracks in flooring or baseboards

- Low water pressure throughout the home

- Damp carpet or a musty smell near the slab




 What causes it


## Why slab leaks happen



Most slab leaks in Orange County homes trace back to aging or corroded copper piping, especially in homes built before PEX became standard. Contributing factors include:




- Corrosion from decades of mineral-rich water exposure

- Pipe abrasion against concrete or rebar over time

- Soil shifting common to coastal and hillside Orange County lots

- Original installation quality issues









 Do this first


## The 30-minute water meter test



This is the most reliable check you can run yourself before calling anyone.




- Turn off every fixture, appliance, irrigation zone, and pool fill valve.

- Write down the meter reading, or take a photo of it.

- Wait 20–30 minutes without using any water.

- Check the meter again. Any change means water is leaving your system somewhere.

- If it moved, close the supply valves to your toilets and irrigation and repeat. If the meter still moves, the leak is most likely in a supply line, which is where acoustic detection comes in.




More detail: [what a moving water meter tells you](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/water-meter-still-moving-everything-off.html).



 Right now


## Should you shut off your water?



It depends on how fast water is escaping.




- **Shut off the main valve now** if water is coming up through the floor, the meter is spinning fast, or a warm spot is spreading.

- **Suspect a hot water line?** Closing the cold-water valve on top of a tank water heater stops flow to the hot side while the rest of the house keeps water. Set the heater to vacation or pilot while it’s off.

- **Meter only creeping?** You usually don’t need to go without water, but don’t wait long. A running leak keeps adding to your bill and to the damage.

 How we find it


## Acoustic detection: listening for the leak itself



Pressurized water escaping a pipe underground makes a distinct sound. Our acoustic equipment isolates that sound through the slab, narrowing the leak's location to a small, specific area before anyone picks up a hammer. It is the same diagnose-first process we bring to older homes across South Orange County — see how we approach [slab leaks in 1970s–1990s Orange County homes](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/south-oc-slab-leak-specialist.html).




 Step 1


### Isolate the Line



Pressure testing confirms which water line is affected, hot or cold, and which zone of the home it runs through.



 Step 2


### Acoustic Sweep



Listening equipment is used across the suspected area to pinpoint the loudest, most consistent point — the leak itself.



 Step 3


### Confirm & Mark



Thermal imaging cross-checks the finding when applicable, and the exact spot is marked for the smallest possible access point.









 Your options after diagnosis


## Repair, reroute, or repipe?






### Spot Repair



Best when there's a single, accessible leak in an otherwise sound plumbing system. The fastest, least invasive fix.





### Reroute



A new line is run around the problem section of pipe, avoiding it entirely rather than repairing it in place.





### Whole-House Repipe



The right call when copper throughout the home is aging or leaks have repeated. We install modern PEX and explain why, honestly.

 Slab leak FAQ


## Common questions



 What causes a slab leak? +


Most slab leaks come from aging or corroded copper pipes, poor original installation, shifting soil, or abrasion where a pipe rubs against concrete or rebar over time.




 How do you find a slab leak without breaking concrete? +


Acoustic listening equipment detects the sound of pressurized water escaping underground, and thermal imaging can reveal temperature differences from hot water leaks — together narrowing the leak to a small, specific area.




 Should I repair, reroute, or repipe? +


A single, accessible leak is often a candidate for a spot repair. Repeated leaks or aging copper throughout the home may make a reroute or full repipe the smarter long-term option — something we discuss after diagnosis, never before.




 What is the difference between a slab leak and an underground water leak? +


A slab leak is an underground leak, but not every underground leak sits under the slab. Supply lines can also run beneath the yard, driveway, or patio. We pressure test and isolate to confirm which line is losing water, then use acoustic listening and line tracing to determine whether the leak is under the foundation or outside it. That distinction changes both the repair and the cost.




 Can you detect a slab leak under tile, hardwood, or laminate? +


Yes. Acoustic listening works through finished flooring because it tracks the sound of pressurized water escaping the pipe rather than looking at the surface. Thermal imaging is most useful on hot water leaks and can be limited by thick flooring, so acoustic detection usually leads. The goal is to narrow the leak to a small area before any flooring or concrete is opened.




 How long does slab leak detection take? +


Most detection visits take one to a few hours, depending on the size of the home and how quickly the source is isolated. We go over your options on the spot. A spot repair is often done the same day or the next, a reroute usually takes one to two days, and a whole-house repipe a few days. More in [how long slab leak repair takes](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/how-long-does-slab-leak-repair-take.html).




 Will my flooring have to come up? +


Not always. Acoustic detection narrows the leak to a small spot, so any opening is as small as possible. A reroute often avoids opening the floor at all by running a new line through the attic or walls. If a spot repair under tile or hardwood is the best option, you will know where the access point is and roughly how big it will be before you decide.




 Should I shut off my water if I think I have a slab leak? +


Shut off the main valve right away if water is coming up through the floor, the meter is spinning fast, or a warm spot is spreading. For a suspected hot water leak, closing the cold-water valve on top of a tank water heater stops flow to the hot side while the rest of the house keeps water. If the meter is only creeping, you usually do not need to go without water, but get it diagnosed soon. See [what to do first](#before-you-call).




 Can a slab leak come back after it is repaired? +


The repaired section itself should not be the problem. But copper tends to corrode throughout a system, so if one section of an older line has failed, other sections of the same age often are not far behind. Two or more leaks within a few years is a common point to consider a reroute or repipe. New PEX greatly lowers the risk, though no plumbing system is leak-proof forever. More in [why some homes get repeat slab leaks](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/multiple-slab-leaks-same-house.html).




 Does homeowners insurance cover slab leak detection and repair? +


Coverage varies by policy and is never something we can promise. Many policies handle the resulting water damage and access to the pipe, while the pipe repair itself is sometimes treated as a maintenance item. A [documented diagnosis](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/insurance/documentation-checklist.html) with the affected line, location, and findings typically makes the claim conversation much easier. See [does insurance cover slab leaks](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/insurance/does-insurance-cover-slab-leaks.html) for more detail.

# Whole-house PEX repiping, explained honestly before it's recommended.



PEX repiping replaces aging, leak-prone copper lines throughout a home with modern, corrosion-resistant piping — but it's only the right call after diagnosis shows it's actually needed.

 The direct answer


## What is whole-house PEX repiping?



PEX repiping means removing or bypassing a home's old metal (usually copper) water lines and replacing them with cross-linked polyethylene (PEX) piping. PEX resists the corrosion and pinhole leaks that plague aging copper, and it's flexible enough to route with fewer joints — fewer joints means fewer future failure points.



We only recommend a full repipe once diagnosis shows it's the smarter long-term choice — not as a default answer to a single leak.



 Why homeowners choose it



- Repeated slab leaks in aging copper lines

- Corrosion visible at multiple fixtures

- Discolored water or metallic taste

- Preparing an older home for resale

- Wanting to stop patching the same system repeatedly








 Copper vs. PEX


## Why PEX has become the modern standard



 Durability

### Corrosion Resistant

PEX doesn't corrode the way copper does, so it removes the pinhole-corrosion failures behind many repeat leaks in aging copper systems.


 Installation

### Fewer Joints

Flexible runs mean fewer fittings behind walls and under slabs — and fewer fittings means fewer places a leak can start.


 Value

### Long-Term Cost

Fewer repeat service calls over the life of the system, even though the upfront investment is real.

 PEX repiping FAQ


## Common questions



 Do I need to repipe my whole house, or just part of it? +


It depends on the age and condition of your existing lines. Sometimes a partial reroute solves the problem; sometimes the whole system is due. We'll tell you which applies after diagnosis, not before.




 How long does a whole-house repipe take? +


It depends on the size of the home, the number of fixtures, and how much wall, ceiling or slab access the new routes need. Many single-family repipes take several working days, plus the city inspection and drywall patching afterward. We give a time estimate for your specific home after the inspection, not before.




 Is PEX safe for drinking water? +


Yes, when the tubing is listed for drinking water. PEX sold for potable plumbing is tested and certified to NSF/ANSI 61 (health effects of drinking-water components). In California, the Building Standards Commission added PEX to the state plumbing code in 2009 and, after a court-ordered environmental review, re-approved it in 2010 (effective January 1, 2011 for the 2010 code). It is now a code-approved water distribution material statewide.




 Do I need a permit to repipe my house? +


In general, yes. Replacing a home's water distribution piping is plumbing alteration work that California cities permit and inspect; in Laguna Niguel that runs through the City's Building & Safety division, which handles permitting and inspections for alterations and remodels. Rules and fees vary by city (and by county for unincorporated areas like Ladera Ranch and Coto de Caza), so confirm with your local building department or ask us which office covers your address.




 What are the limitations of PEX? +


PEX must not be left exposed to sunlight: the Plastics Pipe Institute's PEX design guide says not to store or use PEX where it gets direct or reflected sunlight, and not to install it outdoors unless it is buried or protected from UV. It also has to stay away from heat: the same guide says to keep PEX at least 6 inches from a water heater flue, and recommends flexible metal connectors at least 18 inches long at the water heater. Those are installation details a careful plumber plans for, not reasons to avoid PEX.




 How much does a whole-house repipe cost? +


We don't publish a flat repipe price, because the cost depends on the number of bathrooms and fixtures, single- or two-story layout, whether lines run through the attic or under the slab, drywall patching, and city permit fees. We quote after an inspection of your home.

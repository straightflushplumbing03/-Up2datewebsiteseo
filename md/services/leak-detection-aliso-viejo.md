## Why Aliso Viejo homes leak



**Copper that is now about 30 years old.** Aliso Viejo was built as a master-planned community and became a city in 2001 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliso_Viejo,_California)). The median home was built in 1995 ([City-Stats, 2023 ACS data](https://city-stats.com/california/aliso-viejo/housing)), so many homes still run their original copper water lines. That is younger than most of South Orange County, but old enough that a first pinhole leak is no longer a surprise.



**Condos, townhomes and shared walls.** About 37% of Aliso Viejo housing is multi-family, and about 43% of homes are rented (City-Stats). In attached homes a leak can start in a neighbor's unit or a shared line, so we locate the source and document it before anyone opens a wall. That matters when an HOA, a neighbor, a landlord and two insurers all want to know whose leak it is.



**Under the slab.** A warm spot on the floor or the sound of running water with everything off are typical signs of a leak in a line under the concrete. See [slab leak detection](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/services/slab-leak-detection.html) for how we find it.



**Irrigation and the line from the meter.** Buried service and irrigation lines can leak for months with no sign indoors except the bill. Moulton Niguel Water District lists irrigation leaks among the reasons for a high-bill adjustment, which tells you how often it happens.



## Check for a leak yourself first



Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone, note the reading on your water meter, and check again after 30 minutes. If it moved, water is leaving the system somewhere. Our [water meter test guide](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/water-meter-still-moving-everything-off.html) walks through it, and [why is my water bill so high?](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/why-is-my-water-bill-so-high.html) covers the usual causes.



## Whose side of the meter is the leak on?



[Moulton Niguel Water District](https://www.mnwd.com/operations/) lists Aliso Viejo in its service area and handles water, recycled water and wastewater for most of the city; its number is (949) 831-2500. Your bill names your district. A leak in the street or on the district's side of the meter goes to the district; a leak on your side of the meter, under the slab or inside a wall is generally the owner's to find and fix.



If a leak ran up your bill, Moulton Niguel's [Adjustment Request](https://www.mnwd.com/adjustments/) lists repair of a leak (a toilet, irrigation or a pinhole leak in the home) as a reason, asks for the form within 90 days of the high bill, and says approved adjustments are credited within two billing cycles. If your bill comes from a different district, ask it about its policy. Keep your written findings and the repair invoice.

 How we work


## Find the leak first, then a straight answer on the fix



 1 · Isolate

### Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing shows whether the hot line, cold line or the service line from the meter is losing water, so we know where to look.


 2 · Pinpoint

### Listen, trace and confirm

Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping, electronic locating traces where the pipe runs, and thermal imaging cross-checks hot-water leaks. The spot is marked for the smallest possible opening.


 3 · Decide

### Repair, reroute, or repipe

A single leak in a sound line is usually a spot repair. Repeat leaks in aging copper may make a reroute or repipe the smarter call. We explain the trade-offs after diagnosis, never before.

## Where leaks show up in San Juan Capistrano homes



**Under the slab in 1970s to 1990s homes.** About two-thirds of San Juan Capistrano homes were built between 1970 and 1999 ([NeighborhoodScout](https://www.neighborhoodscout.com/ca/san-juan-capistrano/real-estate)), many on concrete slabs with copper supply lines underneath. A warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water with everything off, or a meter that won't stop are typical signs. More on [slab leak detection](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/services/slab-leak-detection.html).



**Long buried lines on large lots.** On equestrian and other large properties, water lines can run far underground to the house, a barn, a guest unit, troughs and irrigation. A leak there can run for months with no sign indoors except the bill, or a patch that stays green or soggy in a dry month. We test which line is losing water, then trace and listen along it to narrow the leak to one spot.



**Under manufactured homes.** About one in ten homes in the city is a mobile or manufactured home ([NeighborhoodScout](https://www.neighborhoodscout.com/ca/san-juan-capistrano/real-estate)). Their supply lines usually run in the crawlspace, so a leak shows up as soft flooring, a damp smell or water under the skirting rather than a warm slab.



**Inside walls and ceilings, and at worn fixtures.** Supply lines to bathrooms, kitchens and laundry rooms run behind drywall, and in two-story homes a leak upstairs often first appears as a ceiling stain below. San Juan Capistrano's water averages about 12 grains per gallon of hardness ([SMWD 2026 water quality report](https://www.smwd.com/DocumentCenter/View/6351/2026-Water-Quality-Report-ID-9---SJC)), and that scale wears water heaters, shutoff valves and faucet cartridges, so we check those too.



## Check for a leak yourself first



Turn off every faucet, appliance, irrigation zone and any automatic waterers, note the reading on your water meter, and check again after 30 minutes. If it moved, water is leaving the system somewhere. Our [water meter test guide](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/water-meter-still-moving-everything-off.html) walks through it. Since November 15, 2021, the Santa Margarita Water District has run San Juan Capistrano's water service ([Capistrano Dispatch](https://www.picketfencemedia.com/thecapistranodispatch/eye-on-sjc/one-year-later-a-look-at-santa-margarita-water-district-s-acquisition-of-san-juan/article_c7c7baee-a4df-588d-b826-65e81622c1a7.html)), so your SMWD bill history is a good place to spot an unexplained rise in use (see [why is my water bill so high?](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/why-is-my-water-bill-so-high.html)).

 How we work


## Find the leak first, then a straight answer on the fix



 1 · Isolate

### Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing shows whether the hot line, cold line, the service line from the meter or a yard line is losing water, so we know where to look.


 2 · Pinpoint

### Listen, trace and confirm

Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping, electronic locating traces where the pipe runs, and thermal imaging cross-checks hot-water leaks. The spot is marked for the smallest possible opening.


 3 · Decide

### Repair, reroute, or repipe

A single leak in a sound line is usually a spot repair. Repeat leaks in aging copper may make a reroute or repipe the smarter call. We explain the trade-offs after diagnosis, never before.

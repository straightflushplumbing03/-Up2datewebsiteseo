## House leak or yard leak? Check the meter first



Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone and look at the low-flow indicator on your water meter. If it is still moving, water is leaving the system. Then close the main shutoff valve to the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house plumbing: a toilet, a fixture, a line in a wall or under the slab. If it keeps moving with the house valve closed, the leak is between the meter and the house, which usually means irrigation or the buried service line. Our [water meter test guide](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/water-meter-still-moving-everything-off.html) walks through it, and [why is my water bill so high?](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/academy/why-is-my-water-bill-so-high.html) covers the usual causes.

**Irrigation and the line from the meter.** Buried service and irrigation lines can leak for months with no sign indoors except the bill and a soggy patch of lawn.



## Three water districts: whose side is the leak on?



The [City of Lake Forest](https://lakeforestca.gov/node/1409) says most residents are served by Irvine Ranch Water District (which absorbed the former Los Alisos Water District in 2001), with El Toro Water District and Trabuco Canyon Water District serving other parts of the city. Your bill names your district. A leak in the street or on the district's side of the meter goes to them; a leak on your side of the meter is generally the owner's to find and fix.



If you're an IRWD customer and a leak ran up your bill, IRWD's [FAQ](https://irwd.com/faq) says it offers leak adjustments once the leak is found and repaired, and that you are still responsible for paying for the water. Its [home survey page](https://www.irwd.com/get-help/home-survey) says that to make a bill adjustment you complete a short online training and quiz. IRWD customer service is 949-453-5300. El Toro and Trabuco Canyon customers should ask their district about its policy. Keep your written findings and the repair invoice.

 How we work


## Find the leak first, then a straight answer on the fix



 1 · Isolate

### Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing shows whether the hot line, cold line or the service line from the meter is losing water, so we know where to look.


 2 · Pinpoint

### Listen, trace and confirm

Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping, electronic locating traces where the pipe runs, and thermal imaging cross-checks hot-water leaks. The spot is marked for the smallest possible opening.


 3 · Decide

### Repair, reroute, or repipe

A single leak in a sound line is usually a spot repair. Repeat leaks in aging copper may make a reroute or repipe the smarter call. We explain the trade-offs after diagnosis, never before.

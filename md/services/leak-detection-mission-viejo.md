## Where leaks show up in Mission Viejo homes



**Original copper in the older tracts.** Mission Viejo was built as a master-planned community starting in the mid-1960s, and plumbing age varies block to block. Many homes in the older tracts still have their original copper supply lines in the walls and under the slab, which is where pinhole leaks tend to start once pipe has been in service for decades.



**Under the slab.** A warm spot on the floor or the sound of running water with everything off are typical signs of a leak in a line under the concrete. If that is what you are seeing, read [slab leak detection in Mission Viejo](https://straightflushplumbingoc.com/services/slab-leak-detection-mission-viejo.html).



**Inside walls and ceilings.** Supply lines to bathrooms, kitchens and laundry rooms run behind drywall, and in two-story homes a leak upstairs often first appears as a ceiling stain below.



**Irrigation and the line from the meter, especially on sloped lots.** Buried service and irrigation lines can leak for months with no sign indoors except the bill. Moulton Niguel Water District lists irrigation leaks and irrigation timer malfunctions among the reasons for a high-bill adjustment, which tells you how often it happens. A soggy patch, or a spot that stays green in a dry month, can point to it.



## Check for a leak yourself first



Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone, note the reading on your water meter, and check again after 30 minutes. If it moved, water is leaving the system somewhere. ## Find the leak first, then a straight answer on the fix



 1 · Isolate

### Which line is leaking?

Pressure testing shows whether the hot line, cold line or the service line from the meter is losing water, so we know where to look.


 2 · Pinpoint

### Listen, trace and confirm

Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping, electronic locating traces where the pipe runs, and thermal imaging cross-checks hot-water leaks. The spot is marked for the smallest possible opening.


 3 · Decide

### Repair, reroute, or repipe

A single leak in a sound line is usually a spot repair. Repeat leaks in aging or failing copper may make a reroute or repipe the smarter call. We explain the trade-offs after diagnosis, never before.

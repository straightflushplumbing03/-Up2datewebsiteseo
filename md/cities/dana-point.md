## What we know about Dana Point homes



Dana Point became a city on January 1, 1989 and covers about 6.5 square miles of coastline, taking in Capistrano Beach, Lantern Village near the harbor, Niguel Shores and Monarch Beach ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Point,_California); [City of Dana Point](https://danapoint.org/i-want-to-/city-history-facts)). About 65% of homes are single-family, 34% are condos, townhomes or apartments, and under 1% are mobile homes ([City-Stats, 2023 ACS data](https://city-stats.com/california/dana-point/housing)). With a third of homes sharing walls, floors or a roof line, a leak in Dana Point can start in a neighbor’s unit or a shared line, so we locate the source before anyone opens a wall or a ceiling.



Dana Point is served by two water districts. The city says its water system is owned and run by South Coast Water District and Moulton Niguel Water District, and that South Coast Water District runs the sewer system citywide; the city publishes a district boundary map so you can confirm which one serves your address ([City of Dana Point, Utilities](https://www.danapoint.org/department/public-works-engineering/utilities)). Your water bill also shows your district. That matters after a leak, because meter readings, usage history and any leak-related bill adjustment all go through your own district.

 Which water district serves my Dana Point home, and who do I call about a leak? +


It depends on your address.  What are the warning signs of a hidden leak in a Dana Point home? +


An unexplained jump in your water bill, a water meter that keeps moving with everything off, the sound of running water behind a wall, a warm spot on the floor, or dropping pressure. Any one of these in a Dana Point home is worth a professional diagnosis before it becomes water damage.

Decision guide

Repair, reroute, or repipe: how to actually decide.

Once a leak is found and confirmed, the next question is what to do about it. Here's how we think through that decision with every homeowner.

Get Guidance For Your Home

The short answer

Repair the leak in place when it is your first leak, it is in a spot we can reach, and the rest of your plumbing is sound. Reroute when the leak is under the slab but the rest of the system is healthy: the bad section is taken out of service and a new line runs through the attic or walls, so the floor stays intact. Repipe when leaks keep coming back or the original copper is aging throughout the house. The right choice comes from what diagnosis shows, not from a rule of thumb, which is why we locate the leak before recommending anything.

Side by side

What each option involves

Spot Repair

What it is: fixing the leaking section where it is, through one small, targeted access point.

Best fit: a single leak, easy to reach, in an otherwise sound system.

Trade-off: the lowest-cost path, but it does nothing for other aging pipe.

Reroute

What it is: abandoning the leaking line under the slab and running a new line above it, usually through the attic or walls.

Best fit: a slab leak in a home whose other lines are healthy.

Trade-off: a moderate investment; it avoids breaking concrete but means small drywall openings, and it only replaces that one line.

Whole-House Repipe

What it is: replacing the home's water supply piping, typically with PEX.

Best fit: repeat leaks, or aging copper showing corrosion at several fixtures.

Trade-off: the larger investment, but it addresses the root cause once instead of one leak at a time.

Decision checklist

How we think it through with you

1

Choose repair if…This is your first leak, the rest of your plumbing is in good condition, and the leak is in an accessible location.

2

Choose reroute if…The leak is in a hard-to-access spot (like under a slab) but the rest of the system is sound — avoiding the problem section is more practical than repairing it in place.

3

Choose repipe if…You've had multiple leaks, your pipes are original copper from an older build, or discoloration and corrosion show up at multiple fixtures.

4

Consider your timelinePlanning to sell soon? Buyers and inspectors often ask about pipe age and past leaks. Staying long-term? A repipe can end the cycle of repeat repairs.

5

Consider your budgetA spot repair now doesn't rule out a planned repipe later — sometimes the right answer is doing this in stages.

6

When in doubt, askWe'll always tell you honestly which category your situation falls into.

Repair vs. reroute vs. repipe FAQ

It depends on what diagnosis shows. A spot repair fits a first leak in an accessible spot when the rest of the plumbing is sound. A reroute fits when the leak is under the slab but the rest of the system is healthy, so a new line is run around the problem section instead of opening the floor. A whole-house repipe fits when leaks have repeated or the original copper is aging throughout the home.

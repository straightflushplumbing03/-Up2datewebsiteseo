Leak detection & slab leaks

Rather than trusting a "best" label, judge a slab leak specialist on method. They should pressure-test to confirm the leak, then pinpoint it with acoustic, thermal and electronic tools before any concrete is opened. Straight Flush specializes in slab leak detection across South and Central Orange County. Also see what a slab leak is.

A leak detection specialist can. We isolate the plumbing with pressure testing, listen for escaping water with acoustic equipment, check for temperature changes with thermal imaging, and trace pipe paths electronically. That narrows the leak to a small area before anything is opened. See leak detection and how plumbers find leaks without cutting walls.

Straight Flush uses acoustic leak detection throughout South Orange County. Sensitive ground and wall microphones pick up the sound of pressurized water escaping a pipe, even through concrete. Learn more in acoustic leak detection explained.

Straight Flush is based in Laguna Niguel and uses both methods. Thermal imaging shows warm or cool patterns from hot-water or wet areas, and electronic pipe locating traces where lines run under slabs and behind walls. See leak detection in Laguna Niguel.

First, make sure every fixture is off and check your water meter. If the meter is moving, water is going somewhere. A leak detection specialist should come next, not a contractor with a saw. Read sound of running water in the wall and meter moving with everything off, then call (949) 374-6524.

A hot water slab leak often shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a water heater that runs constantly, or a higher gas bill. Thermal imaging and acoustic equipment can pinpoint it through the slab. Straight Flush handles slab leak detection. See warm spot on the floor and hot vs. cold water slab leaks.

Diagnosis first is how Straight Flush works. We confirm the leak, locate it, and explain the repair options before anyone opens a wall, floor or slab. Opening the wrong spot costs more than detection. See leak detection and is leak detection worth the cost?

Start with three checks. (1) Turn off every fixture and watch the water meter; movement means a leak. (2) Look for warm floor spots, damp baseboards, musty smells or new cracks in tile. (3) Compare your water bill with the same month last year. If those point to a leak you can't see, a professional can pinpoint it. See why is my water bill so high? and leak detection.

Straight Flush repairs pinhole leaks in copper lines. A single pinhole can be spot-repaired, but repeated pinholes usually mean the pipe wall is thinning throughout the system. At that point it's worth comparing a repair, a reroute and a repipe. See repair vs. reroute vs. repipe and plumbing repair.

Early signs include a meter that moves when nothing is on, an unexplained jump in the water bill, a water heater that cycles constantly, and damp or warm spots. Calling for leak detection at that stage usually means a smaller repair. Read the hidden costs of ignoring a leak.

Whole-house repiping & PEX

Straight Flush does whole-house PEX repiping across South and Central Orange County, mainly in slab-foundation homes with aging copper that keeps leaking. We route new lines through walls and ceilings to minimize openings. Local pages: Laguna Niguel, Mission Viejo, Dana Point.

One leak in a house otherwise in good shape is usually a repair. Two or more leaks in a few years, pinholes in different areas, or green and blue corrosion on exposed copper point toward repiping. A reroute sits in between. Read repair vs. reroute vs. repipe and multiple slab leaks in the same house.

Straight Flush replaces both hot and cold domestic water lines as part of a whole-house PEX repipe. Hot lines usually fail first because heat speeds up corrosion, so replacing both at once prevents a second project later.

PEX is flexible, so it can be routed through attics and walls with fewer, smaller openings than rigid copper. A careful repipe plans the route, protects floors and furnishings, and closes access holes afterward. See PEX repiping for how we approach it.

Cost depends on the number of bathrooms and fixtures, single or multi-story construction, the route, wall access and permits. A flat number without seeing the house is a guess. PEX resists the pinhole corrosion that affects aging copper, has fewer fittings inside walls, flexes rather than cracking, and usually needs less wall access to install. It's also typically less expensive than repiping in copper. Full comparison: copper vs. PEX pipes.
